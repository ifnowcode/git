How to Remove Viruses and Malware from Your Computer

Important: If you handle sensitive work (banking, medical, business), consider asking a professional for help. These are general steps for home users.

  1. Disconnect from the internet: Turn off Wi‑Fi or unplug the network cable. This helps stop the malware from spreading or sending data.
  2. Back up important files: Copy your most important documents and photos to an external drive or cloud service, if it seems safe to do so.
  3. Boot into Safe Mode: Restart your computer and choose “Safe Mode” (or “Safe Mode with Networking” only if needed). This loads fewer programs and can make removal easier.
  4. Run your antivirus software: Update your antivirus, then run a full system scan. Follow the prompts to quarantine or remove anything it finds.
  5. Use a trusted malware removal tool: Download a reputable anti‑malware scanner from a well‑known vendor and run a full scan to catch anything your antivirus missed.
  6. Remove suspicious programs: Open your system’s “Apps” or “Programs” list and uninstall software you don’t recognize or that appeared around the time problems started.
  7. Check your browser: Remove unknown extensions, reset the homepage and search engine, and clear temporary files and cookies.
  8. Change your passwords: After cleaning the system and reconnecting to the internet, change passwords for email, banking, and other important accounts from a trusted device.
  9. Update your system and apps: Install all operating system updates and update your browser and other key software to close security holes.
  10. Consider a full reset if problems remain: If the computer is still acting strangely, back up your files and perform a factory reset or clean reinstall of the operating system.

Tip: To reduce future risk, keep software updated, use strong passwords, enable multi‑factor authentication, and avoid opening unexpected links or attachments.

Always back up your data in case of corruption by malware or ransomware encryption.

Ransomware attackers usually seek high value targets but will strike lower value targets opportunistically.

Backing up your data might not help if the backup is attached so consider using cloud solutions or dual external backups always leaving one air gapped (unattached). If you only have a single external backup do not leave it attached after backup is done.

Note: An internet search for the specific malware you are infected with can yield custom steps for manual remediation of that particular infection.

Malware includes:

  • Viruses
  • Trojans
  • Worms
  • Ransomware
  • Spyware
  • Adware
  • Scareware
  • Rootkits*

    • ... Or any malicious software.


    * Rootkits may need specialized software to remove and offline scanning to detect.
